Data Analyst Skills in 2026: What Employers Ask For and How to Prove It With a Portfolio

Every year someone declares that AI is about to replace the data analyst, and every year job postings keep asking for the same core skills. What has changed in 2026 is how those skills are tested: less “can you write this query” and more “can you tell when this query is wrong and explain what it means.” Here is what postings ask for, how AI shifted the job, and how to prove your skills with a portfolio.

Isachenko Andrii

Key takeaways
  • SQL tops every analysis of job postings I found; the share varies from about half to over 90% depending on the sample. Excel, a BI tool (Power BI or Tableau) and Python follow.
  • AI has absorbed the mechanical work (first-draft queries, chart drafts). What employers test now is judgment: reviewing AI-generated SQL, validating results and explaining the “so what”.
  • 2–3 end-to-end projects beat a long list of certificates: each needs a business question, messy data, a method and a recommendation.
  • Document how you used AI and how you verified the output — that is a skill, not a confession.
  • Publish where a recruiter can open your work in one click, with a short written insight for each project.

What Job Postings Ask For in 2026

Several published analyses count which skills appear in data analyst job postings. Their numbers differ because their samples, regions and methods differ — but the ranking is consistent.

SkillReported share of postingsComment
SQL~53% to ~92%First or near first in every analysis; 53% in a 1,000-posting study, 60% in 328 descriptions, 92% in 500+ descriptions
Excel81% in one 328-posting sampleNot every study counts it, but where it is counted it ranks at the top
Power BI~29% to ~43%The most-mentioned BI tool in several samples; check postings in your region for Tableau
Python~41% to ~78%Often the “bridge” skill towards analytics engineering
Machine learning~7% of postings; ~22% of entry-level postings in another analysisA plus, rarely a requirement for analysts

Treat these figures as directional: they come from course providers and job-board analyses of differing size and date. The robust conclusion is the order — SQL, Excel, a BI tool, Python, then communication — not any single percentage.

How AI Changed the Job (and What to Show Instead)

AI copilots now write a first-draft join, clean a CSV or sketch a chart in minutes. Recruiter-oriented guides report that hiring has shifted accordingly: candidates are increasingly asked to review and correct an AI-generated query, because that mirrors how SQL is written at work today. The commonly cited conclusion is augmentation rather than replacement: routine query and dashboard drafting get automated, while causal reasoning and business context do not.

That makes query review a portfolio-worthy skill. These are the errors worth checking in any AI-generated SQL:

CheckWhat goes wrong
GrainThe result is per order line but you report per customer, so totals are inflated
Join fan-outA one-to-many join duplicates rows and double-counts revenue
LEFT JOIN + WHEREA filter on the right-hand table silently turns the LEFT JOIN into an INNER JOIN
NULL handlingNULLs drop out of comparisons and averages without any warning
Date boundariesTime zones or an inclusive/exclusive end date shift a day's numbers
ReconciliationThe total does not match a trusted source such as the finance report
✅ In your portfolio, add a short “How I checked this” note to each project: the sanity checks you ran, one bug you caught, and how AI tools helped. It shows exactly the judgment employers are now screening for.

The last row matters more than it looks: a niche turns generic projects into memorable ones. An analyst who understands category management can credibly build ABC-XYZ classifications, price monitoring and assortment analysis — and explain what the business should do next.

Building a Portfolio That Proves It

A portfolio is evidence, not decoration. Two to three complete projects are enough; each should follow the same structure:

  1. Business question — one sentence a manager would recognize.
  2. Data — source, size and license; prefer messy real-world data to a pre-cleaned tutorial set.
  3. Cleaning log — what was wrong (duplicates, nulls, inconsistent categories) and how you fixed it.
  4. Analysis — method and the key queries or code, kept readable.
  5. Insight and recommendation — one paragraph written for a non-technical reader.
  6. Reproducibility — a README that lets someone rerun it.

Project ideas that build on topics from this blog:

  • ABC-XYZ classification of a public retail dataset, with a policy recommendation for each segment.
  • A forecast backtest that scores a model against a seasonal naive baseline using WAPE and bias.
  • A decision-first dashboard with a documented decision brief and an accessibility check.

Host everything where it opens in one click — a public GitHub repository for code and GitHub Pages or your own site for the written story — and link it from your CV and LinkedIn.

Resume and LinkedIn: Describe Outcomes, Not Tools

A list of tools tells a recruiter what you have touched; an outcome tells them what you can do. Use the pattern “Built X in Tool to track Y for Z, enabling decision D” — for example, “Built a Power BI dashboard tracking sales KPIs by region for weekly management reviews.” Where you can, add a measurable result, and only claim what you can explain in an interview.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Python to get an entry-level data analyst job?

Not always: many analyst roles run mainly on SQL, Excel and a BI tool. Python (especially pandas) is increasingly expected, though, and makes you more competitive, so add it once your SQL is solid.

Will AI replace data analysts?

Career guides and recruiters generally describe augmentation rather than replacement: AI speeds up routine SQL and dashboard drafting, while business context, causal reasoning and communication remain human work. This is a trend assessment, not a guarantee.

Should I learn Power BI or Tableau?

In several published samples of job postings Power BI is mentioned more often than Tableau, but the balance varies by region and industry. Check postings in your target market and learn one tool well.

How many portfolio projects do I need?

Two to three complete, end-to-end projects are usually enough, provided each has a business question, a cleaning log, an analysis and a written recommendation.
